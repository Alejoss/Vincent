**Richard Nikolaus Eijiro, Count of Coudenhove-Kalergi** ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language "German language"): *Richard Nikolaus Eijiro Graf von Coudenhove-Kalergi* [^1]; [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language "Japanese language"): リヒャルト・ニコラウス・栄次郎・クーデンホーフ＝カレルギー伯爵, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Japanese "Romanization of Japanese"): *Rihyaruto Nikorausu Eijirō Kūdenhōfu-Karerugī Hakushaku*; 16 November 1894 – 27 July 1972), was a politician, philosopher, ==and [count](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Count "Count") of [Coudenhove-Kalergi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coudenhove-Kalergi "Coudenhove-Kalergi").== A pioneer of [European integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_integration "European integration"), he served as the founding president of the [Paneuropean Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paneuropean_Union "Paneuropean Union") for 49 years. His parents were [Heinrich von Coudenhove-Kalergi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_von_Coudenhove-Kalergi "Heinrich von Coudenhove-Kalergi"), an Austro-Hungarian diplomat, and [Mitsuko Aoyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsuko_Aoyama "Mitsuko Aoyama"), the daughter of an oil merchant, antiques-dealer and major landowner in Tokyo.[^2] His childhood name in Japan was **Eijiro Aoyama** (青山 栄次郎). Being a native Austrian-Hungarian citizen, he became a [Czechoslovak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia "Czechoslovakia") citizen in 1919 and then took French citizenship from 1939 until his death.

His first book, *Pan-Europa*, was published in 1923 and contained a membership form for the Pan-Europa movement, which held its first Congress in 1926 in [Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna "Vienna"). In 1927, [Aristide Briand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristide_Briand "Aristide Briand") was elected honorary president of the Pan-Europa movement. Public figures who attended Pan-Europa congresses included [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein"), [Thomas Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Mann "Thomas Mann") and [Sigmund Freud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigmund_Freud "Sigmund Freud").[^3]

Coudenhove-Kalergi was the first recipient of the [Charlemagne Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne_Prize "Charlemagne Prize") in 1950. The 1972–1973 academic year at the [College of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Europe "College of Europe") was named in his honour. Coudenhove-Kalergi proposed [Beethoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven "Ludwig van Beethoven") 's " [Ode to Joy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ode_to_Joy "Ode to Joy") " as the music for the [European Anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Anthem "European Anthem"). He also proposed a [Europe Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe_Day "Europe Day"), a European postage stamp,[^4] and many artifacts for the movement (e.g. badges and pennants).[^5]

## Family roots

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d8/E%C5%ADropo-placo_C-K.JPG/250px-E%C5%ADropo-placo_C-K.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)

Europa-Platz – Coudenhove-Kalergi in Klosterneuburg, Austria

Coudenhove-Kalergi was the second son of [Heinrich von Coudenhove-Kalergi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_von_Coudenhove-Kalergi "Heinrich von Coudenhove-Kalergi") (1859–1906), an [Austro-Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary "Austria-Hungary") count and diplomat. His mother was [Mitsuko Aoyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsuko_Aoyama "Mitsuko Aoyama") (1874–1941). His father, who spoke sixteen languages and embraced travel as the only means of prolonging life yet died in his forties, had prematurely abandoned a career in the Austrian diplomatic service that took him to [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens "Athens"), [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople "Constantinople"), [Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro "Rio de Janeiro") and Tokyo, to devote himself to study and writing.

Coudenhove-Kalergi's parents met when his mother helped the Austro-Hungarian diplomat after he fell off a horse while riding in Japan. In commenting on their union, [Whittaker Chambers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whittaker_Chambers "Whittaker Chambers") described the future originator of Pan-Europe as "practically a Pan-European organization himself." He elaborated: =="The [Coudenhoves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coudenhove-Kalergi_family "Coudenhove-Kalergi family") were a wealthy [Flemish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flemish_people "Flemish people") family that fled to Austria during the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution "French Revolution").== The [Kalergis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kallergis_family "Kallergis family") were a wealthy Greek family from [Crete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crete "Crete"). The line has been further crossed with Poles, Norwegians, [Balts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balts "Balts"), French and Germans".[^6] ==The [Kalergis family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kallergis_family "Kallergis family") roots claim their descent from [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantium "Byzantium") royalty via== ==[Venetian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice "Venice") aristocracy,== connecting with the [Phokas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phokas_\(Byzantine_family\) "Phokas (Byzantine family)") imperial dynasty. In 1300, Coudenhove-Kalergi's ancestor Alexios Phokas-Kalergis signed the treaty that made Crete a dominion of [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice "Republic of Venice").

During his childhood, Coudenhove-Kalergi's mother had read aloud to him *[Momotarō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momotar%C5%8D "Momotarō")* and other Japanese fairy tales.[^7]

## Youth and education

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/Pob%C4%9B%C5%BEovice_z%C3%A1mek%283%29.jpg/250px-Pob%C4%9B%C5%BEovice_z%C3%A1mek%283%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)

The Poběžovice (Ronsperg) castle, his childhood home. Damaged during the Second World War, the repairs were overseen by a German from Japan Masumi Schmidt-Muraki.

==Coudenhove-Kalergi spent his adolescence on [Bohemian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemia "Bohemia") family estates in [Ronsperg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronsperg "Ronsperg"),== known today as [Poběžovice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pob%C4%9B%C5%BEovice "Poběžovice"). His father personally taught his two sons Russian and Hungarian and toughened them both physically and morally. He took them on long walks in all weather, made them sleep on straw mattresses and take cold showers, and taught them to shoot and fence so well that no one would ever dare challenge them. He also took them to [Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_\(liturgy\) "Mass (liturgy)") every Sunday. On every [Good Friday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Friday "Good Friday"), as the [liturgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Rite "Roman Rite") came to the exhortation "oremus et pro perfidis Judaeis" (" [Let us also pray for the perfidious Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Friday_Prayer_for_the_Jews "Good Friday Prayer for the Jews") "), the old count allegedly rose and walked out of the church in a protest against this expression of [antisemitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism "Antisemitism").[^6]

Coudenhove-Kalergi studied at the [Augustiner-Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustiner-Gymnasium_Brixen?action=edit&redlink=1 "Augustiner-Gymnasium Brixen (page does not exist)") in [Brixen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brixen "Brixen") before attending the [Theresianische Akademie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theresianische_Akademie "Theresianische Akademie") in Vienna from 1908 until 1913. He obtained his doctorate in philosophy with a thesis on *Die Objectivität als Grundprinzip der Moral* (Objectivity as the Fundamental Principle of Morals) in 1917 from the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna "University of Vienna").

During his student years, ==Coudenhove-Kalergi married the famous Jewish Viennese actress [Ida Roland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ida_Roland "Ida Roland")== in April 1915. His marriage to a [divorcée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divorcee "Divorcee") thirteen years his senior, and a [commoner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commoner "Commoner"), caused a temporary split with his family. His mother Mitsuko did not accept Ida, considering her a "beggar living on the riverbank," [^8] a traditional Japanese point of view against actors and performers. His mother, as head of the family, banned him from the family temporarily, but relented when Coudenhove-Kalergi became renowned for his pan-European concept.

## Personal philosophy

He was Aristocratic in his origins and this reflected in his ideas, Coudenhove-Kalergi identified and collaborated with such politicians as [Engelbert Dollfuss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engelbert_Dollfuss "Engelbert Dollfuss"), [Kurt Schuschnigg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Schuschnigg "Kurt Schuschnigg"), [Otto von Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_von_Habsburg "Otto von Habsburg"), [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill "Winston Churchill"), and [Charles de Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle "Charles de Gaulle").[^9] His ideal political constituent was a [gentleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentleman "Gentleman") who must respect and protect ladies, a person adhering to honesty, fair play, courtesy, and [rational discourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialectics "Dialectics").[^10] [^11]

## Pan-European political activist

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Richard_Coudenhove-Calergi_a_Thomas_Mann%2C_Berl%C3%ADn_1930.jpg/250px-Richard_Coudenhove-Calergi_a_Thomas_Mann%2C_Berl%C3%ADn_1930.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)

Thomas Mann, Ida Roland and her husband Richard Coudenhove-Kalergi in the second Pan-European Congress in Sing-Akademie zu Berlin on 17 May 1930

Coudenhove-Kalergi is recognized as the founder of the first popular movement for a united Europe. His intellectual influences ranged from [Immanuel Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant "Immanuel Kant"), [Rudolf Kjellén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Kjell%C3%A9n "Rudolf Kjellén") and [Oswald Spengler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Spengler "Oswald Spengler") to [Arthur Schopenhauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Schopenhauer "Arthur Schopenhauer") and [Friedrich Nietzsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Nietzsche "Friedrich Nietzsche"). In politics, he was an enthusiastic supporter of the [Fourteen Points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourteen_Points "Fourteen Points") made by [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson "Woodrow Wilson") on 8 January 1918 and [pacifist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacifism "Pacifism") initiatives of [Kurt Hiller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Hiller "Kurt Hiller"). I==n December 1921, he joined the [Masonic lodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masonic_lodge "Masonic lodge") "Humanitas" in Vienna.==[^12] According to [Stephen Dorril](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Dorril "Stephen Dorril"), in 1922, he co-founded the Pan-European Union (PEU), as "the only way of guarding against an eventual world hegemony by Russia." [^13] In 1923, he published a manifesto entitled *Pan-Europa*, each copy containing a membership form which invited the reader to become a member of the [Pan-Europa movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Paneuropean_Union "International Paneuropean Union"). He favored social democracy as an improvement on "the feudal aristocracy of the sword" but his ambition was to create a conservative society that superseded democracy with "the social aristocracy of the spirit." [^14] ==European freemason lodges supported his movement, including the lodge Humanitas.==[^15] *Pan-Europa* was translated into the languages of European countries (excluding Italian, which edition was not published at that time), the constructed language [Occidental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interlingue "Interlingue") [^16] and a multitude of other languages, except for Russian.[^17]

==In April 1924, Coudenhove-Kalergi founded the journal *Paneuropa* (1924–1938) of which he was editor and principal author.== The next year he started publishing his main work, the *Kampf um Paneuropa* (The fight for Paneuropa, 1925–1928, three volumes). In 1926, the first Congress of the Pan-European Union was held in Vienna and the 2,000 delegates elected Coudenhove-Kalergi as president of the Central Council, a position he held until his death in 1972.

His original vision was for a world divided into only five states: a United States of Europe that would link continental countries with French and Italian possessions in Africa; a Pan-American Union encompassing North and South Americas; the British Commonwealth circling the globe; the USSR spanning Eurasia; and a Pan-Asian Union whereby Japan and China would control most of the Pacific. To him, the only hope for a Europe devastated by war was to federate along lines that the Hungarian-born Romanian [Aurel Popovici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurel_Popovici "Aurel Popovici") and others had proposed for the dissolved multinational Empire of Austria-Hungary. According to Coudenhove-Kalergi, Pan-Europe would encompass and extend a more flexible and more competitive Austria-Hungary, with English serving as the world language, spoken by everyone in addition to their native tongue. He believed that individualism and socialism would learn to cooperate instead of compete, and urged that capitalism and communism cross-fertilise each other just as the Protestant Reformation had spurred the Catholic Church to regenerate itself.[^18]

Coudenhove-Kalergi attempted to enlist prominent European politicians in his pan-European cause. He offered the presidency of the Austrian branch of the Pan-European Union to [Ignaz Seipel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignaz_Seipel "Ignaz Seipel"), who accepted the offer unhesitatingly and rewarded his beneficiary with an office in the old Imperial palace in Vienna. Coudenhove-Kalergi had less success with [Tomáš Masaryk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Garrigue_Masaryk "Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk"), who referred him to his uncooperative Prime Minister [Edvard Beneš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edvard_Bene%C5%A1 "Edvard Beneš"). However, the idea of pan-Europe elicited support from politicians as diverse as the Italian anti-Fascist politician [Carlo Sforza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Sforza "Carlo Sforza") and the German President of the [Reichsbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichsbank "Reichsbank") under [Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler"), [Hjalmar Schacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hjalmar_Schacht "Hjalmar Schacht"). Although Coudenhove-Kalergi found himself unable to sway [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini "Benito Mussolini"), his ideas influenced [Aristide Briand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristide_Briand "Aristide Briand") through his speech in favour of a European Union in the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations "League of Nations") on 8 September 1929, as well as his famous 1930 "Memorandum on the Organisation of a Regime of European Federal Union." [^19]

Coudenhove-Kalergi proposed Beethoven's "Ode to Joy" as the [Anthem of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthem_of_Europe "Anthem of Europe") in 1929,[^4] which he later proposed in 1955 as Anthem for the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union"). In 1930, he proposed a Europe Day in May [^4] and in 1932 he proposed to celebrate every 17 May, the anniversary of Aristide Briand's "Memorandum" being published in 1930.[^20]

His Pan-Europeanism earned vivid loathing from Adolf Hitler, who excoriated its pacifism and mechanical economism and belittled its founder as "a bastard." [^21] [^22] Hitler's view of Coudenhove-Kalergi was that the "rootless, cosmopolitan, and elitist half-breed" was going to repeat the historical mistakes of Coudenhove ancestors who had served the [House of Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Habsburg "House of Habsburg").[^23] In 1928, Hitler wrote about his political opponent in his *[Zweites Buch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zweites_Buch "Zweites Buch")*, describing him as "Allerweltsbastard (commonplace bastard) Coudenhove".[^24] [^25]

Hitler argued in his 1928 *[Secret Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler's_Secret_Book "Hitler's Secret Book")* that they are unfit for the future defense of Europe against America. As America fills its North American [lebensraum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebensraum "Lebensraum"), "the natural activist urge that is peculiar to young nations will turn outward." But then "a pacifist-democratic Pan-European hodgepodge state" would not be able to oppose the United States, as it is "according to the conception of that commonplace bastard, Coudenhove-Kalergi..." [^26] Nazi criticism and propaganda against Coudenhove-Kalergi, and his European worldview, would decades later form the basis of the racist [Kalergi plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalergi_plan "Kalergi plan") [conspiracy theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_theory "Conspiracy theory").[^27]

Nazis considered the Pan-European Union to be under the control of [Freemasonry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freemasonry "Freemasonry").[^28] In 1938, a [Nazi propaganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_propaganda "Nazi propaganda") book, *Die Freimaurerei: Weltanschauung, Organisation und Politik* (*Freemasonry: Its World View, Organization and Policies*), was released in German, which targeted Coudenhove-Kalergi's membership of Freemasonry, the organization [suppressed by Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suppression_of_Freemasonry#Nazi_Germany_and_occupied_Europe "Suppression of Freemasonry").[^29] Coudenhove-Kalergi had already left the Viennese Masonic Lodge in 1926 to avoid the criticism that occurred at that time of the relationship between the Pan-European movement and Freemasonry.

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/Casablanca%2C_Paul_Henreid.JPG/250px-Casablanca%2C_Paul_Henreid.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)

Paul Henreid as Victor Laszlo in the cinematic trailer of Casablanca

After [the annexation of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss "Anschluss") by [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany") in 1938, Coudenhove-Kalergi fled to [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia "Czechoslovakia"), and thence to France. As France fell to Germany in 1940, he escaped to the United States by way of Switzerland and Portugal. When he passed a few days after the successful escape to the United States, he listened to the radio announcing the possibility that he had died.[^30] During World War II, he continued his call for the unification of Europe along the Paris-London axis. His wartime politics and adventures served as the real life basis for fictional Resistance hero Victor Laszlo, the [Paul Henreid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Henreid "Paul Henreid") character in *[Casablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca_\(film\) "Casablanca (film)")*.[^31]

Coudenhove-Kalergi published his work *Crusade for Paneurope* in 1944. His appeal for the unification of Europe enjoyed some support from [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill "Winston Churchill"), [Allen Dulles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen_Dulles "Allen Dulles"), and ["Wild Bill" Donovan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Joseph_Donovan "William Joseph Donovan").[^32] After the announcement of the [Atlantic Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Charter "Atlantic Charter") on 14 August 1941, he composed a memorandum entitled "Austria's Independence in the light of the Atlantic Charter" and sent it to [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill "Winston Churchill") and [Franklin Delano Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Delano_Roosevelt "Franklin Delano Roosevelt"). In his position statement, Coudenhove-Kalergi took up the goals of the charter and recommended himself as head of government in exile. Both Churchill and Roosevelt distanced themselves from this document. From 1942 until his return to France in 1945, he taught at the [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University "New York University"), which appointed him professor of history in 1944. At the same university Professor [Ludwig von Mises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_von_Mises "Ludwig von Mises") studied currency problems for Coudenhove-Kalergi's movement.[^33]

On 22 July 1943, Nazis deprived him of his Doctor of Philosophy degree from the University of Vienna, with the racist argument, that as a "Jew" he was not considered worthy of an academic degree from a German university (" *eines akademischen Grades einer deutschen Hochschule unwürdig* ") – even though he was not Jewish nor was his family Jewish. His doctorate degree was only regranted on 15 May 1955, six years after the founding of the Federal Republic of Germany and thus ten years after the end of [Nazism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism "Nazism").[^34]

The end of the World War II inaugurated a revival of pan-European hopes. In the winter of 1945, [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman "Harry S. Truman") read an article in the December issue of *[Collier's magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collier's "Collier's")* that Coudenhove-Kalergi posted about the integration of Europe. His article impressed Truman, and it was adopted to the United States' official policy.[^35] Winston Churchill's celebrated speech of 19 September 1946 to the Academic Youth in [Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich "Zurich") commended "the exertions of the Pan-European Union which owes so much to Count Coudenhove-Kalergi and which commanded the services of the famous French patriot and statesman Aristide Briand." [^36] ==In November 1946 and the spring of 1947, Coudenhove-Kalergi circulated an enquiry addressed to members of European parliaments. This enquiry resulted in the founding of the [European Parliamentary Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Parliamentary_Union "European Parliamentary Union") (EPU),== a nominally private organization that held its preliminary conference on 4–5 July at Gstaad, Switzerland, and followed it with its first full conference from 8 to 12 September. Speaking at the first EPU conference, Coudenhove-Kalergi argued that the constitution of a wide market with a stable currency was the vehicle for Europe to reconstruct its potential and take the place it deserves within the concert of Nations. On less guarded occasions he was heard to advocate a revival of [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne "Charlemagne") 's empire.[^37] In 1950 he received the first annual *[Karlspreis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlspreis "Karlspreis")* (Charlemagne Award), given by the German city of [Aachen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aachen "Aachen") to people who contributed to the European idea and European peace. In Japan, a politician [Ichirō Hatoyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichir%C5%8D_Hatoyama "Ichirō Hatoyama") was influenced by Coudenhove-Kalergi's fraternity in his book *The Totalitarian State Against Man*. It was translated into Japanese by Hatoyama and published in 1952. ==Coudenhove-Kalergi was appointed the honorary chairman of the fraternal youth association that Hatoyama, with the influence of his book, had established in 1953.==

In 1955, he proposed the [Beethoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven "Ludwig van Beethoven") 's " [Ode to Joy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ode_to_Joy "Ode to Joy") " as the music for the [European Anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Anthem "European Anthem"),[^38] a suggestion that the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe "Council of Europe") took up 16 years later.

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/Plaque_Place_Richard_Coudenhove_Kalergi_-_Paris_XVI_%28FR75%29_-_2021-08-18_-_1.jpg/250px-Plaque_Place_Richard_Coudenhove_Kalergi_-_Paris_XVI_%28FR75%29_-_2021-08-18_-_1.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)

Plaque at Place Richard de Coudenhove-Kalergi in Paris

In the 1960s, Coudenhove-Kalergi urged Austria to pursue "an active policy of peace", as a "fight against the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War") and its continuation, the atomic war". He advocated Austrian involvement in world politics in order to keep the peace, as "active neutrality". He continued his advocacy of European unification in memoranda circulated to the governments of the Federal Republic of Germany, France, the United Kingdom, and Italy. He recommended negotiations between the [European Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Community "European Community") and the [European Free Trade Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Free_Trade_Association "European Free Trade Association") towards forming a "European customs union" that would be free of political and military connections, but would eventually adopt a monetary union.

## Views on race and religion

In his 1925 book *Practical Idealism*, Coudenhove-Kalergi theorized that the historical development causing the death of European hereditary social classes would lead to an all-encompassing [race of the future](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_of_the_future "Race of the future") made up of "Eurasian- [Negroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negroid "Negroid") \[s\]", which would replace "the diversity of peoples" and "\[t\]oday's races and classes" with a "diversity of individuals".[^39] [^40]

In an interview in the first Pan-European Congress in 1926, he expressed the support of Jews by the pan-European movement and the benefits to Jews with the elimination of racial hatred and economic rivalry brought by the United States of Europe.[^41]

In 1932, Coudenhove-Kalergi composed, and had his publishing house reissue, a preface for a new edition of his father [Heinrich von Coudenhove-Kalergi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_von_Coudenhove-Kalergi "Heinrich von Coudenhove-Kalergi") 's condemnation of antisemitism in his later life. In 1933, he responded to the ascendance of [Nazism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism "Nazism") by collaborating with [Heinrich Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Mann "Heinrich Mann"), [Arthur Holitscher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Holitscher "Arthur Holitscher"), [Lion Feuchtwanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion_Feuchtwanger "Lion Feuchtwanger"), and [Max Brod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Brod "Max Brod") in writing and publishing the pamphlet *Gegen die Phrase vom jüdischen Schädling* ("Against the Phrase ' [Jewish Parasite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Parasite "Jewish Parasite") '").[^42]

## Journeys to Japan

### Return to Japan

The Pan-European idea influenced a young Japanese diplomat – in the future, the president of [Kajima Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kajima_Corporation "Kajima Corporation") – [Morinosuke Kajima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morinosuke_Kajima?action=edit&redlink=1 "Morinosuke Kajima (page does not exist)") during his residence in Berlin in 1922.[^43] Coudenhove-Kalergi formed a friendship with Kajima and then asked him to translate the book *Pan-Europa* into Japanese.[^43] ==He proposed Pan-Asia to Kajima and promised to give [Dutch East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_Indies "Dutch East Indies") as their friendship after the realization of the task to establish== Pan-Asia.[^43] Kajima published *Pan-Europa* in Japanese in 1927. In 1930 Kajima retired from the [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Japan\) "Ministry of Foreign Affairs (Japan)") to become [MP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_parliament "Member of parliament"). ==His ambition to become an MP was due to Coudenhove-Kalergi's influence.==[^44] In 1970–1971 he published the complete works of Coudenhove-Kalergi from Kajima Institute Publishing that was established by Morinosuke Kajima. He respected Coudenhove-Kalergi over a lifetime, dreaming of the realization of Pan-Asia.[^43]

In Japan, the Pan-European idea also influenced a journalist [Yoshinori Maeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshinori_Maeda?action=edit&redlink=1 "Yoshinori Maeda (page does not exist)"), the president of [NHK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHK "NHK"). He became a pioneer of [Asia-Pacific Broadcasting Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific_Broadcasting_Union "Asia-Pacific Broadcasting Union") with the image of *Pan-Europa* that he read in his student days.[^45]

In 1953 [Ichirō Hatoyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichir%C5%8D_Hatoyama "Ichirō Hatoyama") established [Yuai Youth Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuai_Youth_Association?action=edit&redlink=1 "Yuai Youth Association (page does not exist)") (later Yuai Association), the fraternal association as the successor of fraternity that Coudenhove-Kalergi mentioned in *The Totalitarian State Against Man*. The Japanese word *yūai* ([友愛](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%8F%8B%E6%84%9B "wikt:友愛")) has several meanings but especially the word used by Hatoyama means *fraternity* and in German *[brüderlichkeit](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Br%C3%BCderlichkeit "wikt:Brüderlichkeit")*.[^46] It can also be considered equivalent to " [Liberty, Equality, Fraternity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libert%C3%A9,_%C3%A9galit%C3%A9,_fraternit%C3%A9 "Liberté, égalité, fraternité") " (Brotherhood), the motto of the French Republic. An educator [Kaoru Hatoyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaoru_Hatoyama "Kaoru Hatoyama") became the second president of the association after her husband Ichirō, the first president, died in 1959.

In 1967 Coudenhove-Kalergi was awarded the Kajima Peace Award, and was invited to Japan by Morinosuke Kajima as the president of Kajima Institute of International Peace, Yoshinori Maeda as the president of NHK, and Kaoru Hatoyama as the president of Yuai Youth Association. Together with his second wife Alexandra in a wheelchair,[^47] Coudenhove-Kalergi stayed in Japan from 26 October to 8 November. He was also accompanied by his young brother [Gerolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerolf_Coudenhove-Kalergi?action=edit&redlink=1 "Gerolf Coudenhove-Kalergi (page does not exist)") 's daughter [Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Coudenhove-Kalergi "Barbara Coudenhove-Kalergi").[^48] Richard Coudenhove-Kalergi was also awarded *First Order of the Sacred Treasure* of Japan. He was granted an audience with the Emperor [Hirohito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirohito "Hirohito"), [Empress Kōjun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empress_K%C5%8Djun "Empress Kōjun"), their son Crown Prince [Akihito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akihito "Akihito") to whom he had presented his book in 1953 in Switzerland, and Crown Princess [Michiko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empress_Michiko "Empress Michiko"). This time, he had returned to Japan for the first time since his childhood 71 years earlier. He gave several lectures and met various leaders. Coudenhove-Kalergi spent 2 weeks in Japan as a guest of Japanese TV, radio, newspaper, magazines and other media.[^49] While in Japan, Coudenhove-Kalergi specifically asked for a meeting with the president of [Soka Gakkai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soka_Gakkai "Soka Gakkai"), [Daisaku Ikeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisaku_Ikeda "Daisaku Ikeda"), as Coudenhove-Kalergi had been interested in Ikeda's work for many years.[^50]

## Death

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cb/Coudenhove-Park.Hofpavillon.U4hietzing.A.JPG/250px-Coudenhove-Park.Hofpavillon.U4hietzing.A.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)

Coudenhove-Park in Hietzing, Vienna

According to a masonic report, Coudenhove-Kalergi died of a stroke.[^51] His secretary, however, indicated that Coudenhove-Kalergi possibly committed suicide. In the memoir his secretary wrote, she said his death had been kept secret so as not to disappoint those who considered him to be the great visionary of European integration.[^52] Coudenhove-Kalergi was the head of the Pan-European Union until his death. [Otto von Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_von_Habsburg "Otto von Habsburg") succeeded him as president.

Coudenhove-Kalergi is buried at [Gruben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruben/Meiden "Gruben/Meiden") near [Gstaad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gstaad "Gstaad").[^53] His grave, covered with [wild grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_grape "Wild grape"), is located in a [Japanese rock garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_rock_garden "Japanese rock garden") in the [Swiss Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Alps "Swiss Alps"). Upon the grave is the French epitaph " *Pionnier des États-Unis d'Europe* " (Pioneer of the United States of Europe).[^54]

Coudenhove-Kalergi was married three times: to [Ida Roland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ida_Roland "Ida Roland") (1881–1951), to Alexandra Gräfin von Tiele-Winckler (1896–1968), and to Melanie Benatzky-Hoffmann (1909–1983). His known children were Ida's daughter Erika and Alexandra's son Alexander, both of whom were his step-children.[^55]
